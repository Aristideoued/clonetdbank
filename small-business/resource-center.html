Title: Profit Plan

Content

Every business wants to improve profits but deciding on the right course-of-action to get there can be tricky.

Thankfully, with a little planning you can start taking action straight away that will add up to one impressive performance hike for your business.

It’s less about trying to double your sales and more about small incremental improvements over your whole business all at the same time.

The easiest way to get the ball rolling is to look at improving the four key profit areas, starting with sales leads.

More sales leads simply means more sales opportunities.

And we’re not just talking about hiring a team of telemarketers.

It could be just getting one extra possible client out of every ten.

Next, is sales conversions.

The art of sealing the deal by turning those leads into regular customers.

What can you offer to get someone who would have said no to say yes?

You’ll then need to look at cost reduction.

Those areas where you can safely trim costs without effecting your quality and service levels.

Last, but not least is your overall efficiency, looking at ways to complete a task more easily or in a smarter way, or faster or with less waste.

How can you be more productive?

Your profit improvement plan combines all these elements into a practical action plan of ideas that you can easily achieve without having to change your business substantially.

Title: Set a price

Content

Setting a price is like walking a tightrope.

It's a balancing act between pricing too low – and not making a profit and pricing too high and forcing your potential customers to look elsewhere.

Thankfully, you can take the guesswork out of pricing and lower the risk by simply calculating the fixed and variable costs of getting your product to market compared to where you want to be positioned and price accordingly.

For example: take this bicycle: it's purchased from a supplier, delivered to a store, and assembled prior to sale.

The bicycle is $200 and delivery is an extra $50.

Now, add up the costs of assembling and storing it.

The costs can soon add up but, there's a bit more to it – it's called mark-up.

Otherwise known as your profit margin.

You need to add a dollar figure to the bike for all your hard work and risk you are taking being in business.

And now there is the competition to worry about.

They already have prices in the marketplace that everyone will be aware of.

What you do will depend on how you position your business in the market.

Will you be a cost-leader, or do your products and services demand a premium?

So setting a price is a balance of what it actually cost you to deliver the product or service, adding a profit margin and finally where you want to sit compared to everyone else.

Just remember, a low price isn’t everything, your unique skills, experience, and overall approach to customer service are just as important.

Title: Raising capital

Content

So, you’ve dreamt up the next big thing.

But to make it work you need cash.

Thankfully, there are plenty of options to explore that don’t involve signing away 95% of your idea to the first shrewd investor on the scene.

Some of the more tried-and-true ways of raising capital include personal savings and loans, or loans from family and friends.

But before you ask Aunty Doris to finance your new work vehicle there are other options to consider.

Your bank is a great place to start.

As well as having the cash to help you out they can also help you create a plan to manage your finances long-term.

Next up are angel investors.

These are often entrepreneurs or retired business owners who are looking for exciting enterprises to support and mentor.

You’ll need to keep your eyes open to find them.

Industry associations and networking events are great places to start looking other options for additional capital include Venture capitalists who seek a return on investment by exiting your business after it is successful.

Or government grants, or even new tactics such as crowd funding.

Regardless of who you are pitching to make sure you match the source of the capital to what you want to use it for.

You wouldn't want to ask a venture capitalist to fund a new vehicle or a bank to fund an unproven idea without any security.

Finally, to attract funding you'll have to prove you are worth the investment having a solid business plan is your best start.

Title: Is there a market

Content

There’s nothing like blue sky thinking.

It’s where every business starts, with a light bulb moment and the spark of invention.

But what if your idea is less blue sky and more pie in the sky.

If when it comes to turning a profit it might be more of a case of ‘pigs might fly’.

This is the question every entrepreneur faces.

Do they have a great business idea or just a flight of fancy?

To find out entrepreneurs use feasibility studies to see if their business ideas will take wings and fly.

They do this by studying whether there is a market for their product or service idea before they commit money, time and resources to a project that could rocket them to profit or go down like a lead balloon.

A feasibility study tries to answer questions such as, “Who’ll buy the product/service?”, “How much money would they be willing to pay?”, “How often would they be likely to buy it?”.

Feasibility studies often involve many different types of research.

From statistical research that estimates the potential size of a target market, to product testing that tries to determine whether the business’s unique selling points gel with it’s intended customers.

Feasibility studies also look into the rate of return possible from investing money in a business idea and the power of competitors who already exist in the market or who might try to replicate your success and ride on your coat tails.

But ultimately, what feasibility studies come down to is finding out whether people you don’t know will spend money with you.

It’s about brutal honesty, finding out the hard, unbiased truth about whether your business idea has the potential to get off the ground before you strap yourself in for the ride.

Title: Reducing the risk

Content

So, you want to start your business, but are worried about the risks involved?

Before you lose too much sleep, let’s look at some common start up risks and ways to reduce the risk and get you closer to starting up.

Business risk generally falls into three categories: Market risk, internal risk and last but not least, financial risk.

Market risk centers on the possibility that your products and services won’t sell as you hope.

It’s a common fear, but the good news is you can almost remove the risk entirely with some basic market research and customer profiling.

Internal risks are usually based around your capacity to deliver goods on time and to a high standard and having staff that are experienced enough to handle every day challenges.

To reduce these risks you’ll need to identify any capability issues before starting up.

Finally, but arguably most important, is financial risk.

The fear you’ll struggle to turn a profit or unforeseen costs will halt your progress.

Thankfully, good financial planning can reduce these risks allowing you to breathe easier and focus on what matters, growing your business.

Just remember, you don’t need to go it alone so surround yourself with people who have the experience and expertise to help you turn your plans into reality.

Title: Cash flow forecasts

Content

Cash flow forecasting is a vital cog in your financial machine.

It keeps you aware of your financial health while you focus on what matters – running your business.

So, what’s it all about?

It’s basic math – but don’t worry there won’t be any pop quizzes here.

It’s simply about forecasting the amount of cash going into your business minus the amount going out as expenses. Easy.

Well, sort of easy.

If you are in business, then you have last years’ results and some past experience to help you predict what might happen, but if you’re a new start-up then forecasting sales is the hardest part.

Crystal ball gazing might be more accurate.

You will need to make some assumptions.

However, once you have an idea of how much you have left over it gets easier to forecast your likely cashflow for the coming months.

The result? You anticipate cash flow problems before they happen and you know exactly how much cash you have available to take advantage of growth opportunities.

Breathe a sigh of relief.

Title: Profit Plan

Content

Every business wants to improve profits but deciding on the right course-of-action to get there can be tricky.

Thankfully, with a little planning you can start taking action straight away that will add up to one impressive performance hike for your business.

It’s less about trying to double your sales and more about small incremental improvements over your whole business all at the same time.

The easiest way to get the ball rolling is to look at improving the four key profit areas, starting with sales leads.

More sales leads simply means more sales opportunities.

And we’re not just talking about hiring a team of telemarketers.

It could be just getting one extra possible client out of every ten.

Next, is sales conversions.

The art of sealing the deal by turning those leads into regular customers.

What can you offer to get someone who would have said no to say yes?

You’ll then need to look at cost reduction.

Those areas where you can safely trim costs without effecting your quality and service levels.

Last, but not least is your overall efficiency, looking at ways to complete a task more easily or in a smarter way, or faster or with less waste.

How can you be more productive?

Your profit improvement plan combines all these elements into a practical action plan of ideas that you can easily achieve without having to change your business substantially.

Title: Raising capital

Content

So, you’ve dreamt up the next big thing.

But to make it work you need cash.

Thankfully, there are plenty of options to explore that don’t involve signing away 95% of your idea to the first shrewd investor on the scene.

Some of the more tried-and-true ways of raising capital include personal savings and loans, or loans from family and friends.

But before you ask Aunty Doris to finance your new work vehicle there are other options to consider.

Your bank is a great place to start.

As well as having the cash to help you out they can also help you create a plan to manage your finances long-term.

Next up are angel investors.

These are often entrepreneurs or retired business owners who are looking for exciting enterprises to support and mentor.

You’ll need to keep your eyes open to find them.

Industry associations and networking events are great places to start looking other options for additional capital include Venture capitalists who seek a return on investment by exiting your business after it is successful.

Or government grants, or even new tactics such as crowd funding.

Regardless of who you are pitching to make sure you match the source of the capital to what you want to use it for.

You wouldn't want to ask a venture capitalist to fund a new vehicle or a bank to fund an unproven idea without any security.

Finally, to attract funding you'll have to prove you are worth the investment having a solid business plan is your best start.

Title: Set a price

Content

Setting a price is like walking a tightrope.

It's a balancing act between pricing too low – and not making a profit and pricing too high and forcing your potential customers to look elsewhere.

Thankfully, you can take the guesswork out of pricing and lower the risk by simply calculating the fixed and variable costs of getting your product to market compared to where you want to be positioned and price accordingly.

For example: take this bicycle: it's purchased from a supplier, delivered to a store, and assembled prior to sale.

The bicycle is $200 and delivery is an extra $50.

Now, add up the costs of assembling and storing it.

The costs can soon add up but, there's a bit more to it – it's called mark-up.

Otherwise known as your profit margin.

You need to add a dollar figure to the bike for all your hard work and risk you are taking being in business.

And now there is the competition to worry about.

They already have prices in the marketplace that everyone will be aware of.

What you do will depend on how you position your business in the market.

Will you be a cost-leader, or do your products and services demand a premium?

So setting a price is a balance of what it actually cost you to deliver the product or service, adding a profit margin and finally where you want to sit compared to everyone else.

Just remember, a low price isn’t everything, your unique skills, experience, and overall approach to customer service are just as important.
